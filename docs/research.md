# Training app "brain": research notes (2026-10-31)

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[R]**: research (meta-analysis / RCT)
- **[E]**: expert consensus or expert coaching texts (Delphi panels, evidence-based coaches)
- **[C]**: community convention (popular programs, Reddit wikis, app defaults)

Lifter: adult male painter (active job), started Aug 2026, about 6 weeks of LP, main lifts roughly 40–70 kg, trains 2–4×/week with occasional 1–2-week gaps.
Targets for about Aug 2028: SQ 155, BP 130, DL 210, OHP 80, 20 strict pull-ups.

## 1. Session structure and frequency

### Evidence
- **Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016** (Sports Med, meta-analysis): training a muscle at least 2×/week gave more hypertrophy than 1×/week. Whether 3× beats 2× was unresolved. [R] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/
- **Ralston et al. 2018** (Sports Med Open): with volume equated, frequency had no significant effect on strength (ES 0.03). Without volume equating, higher frequency tended to give more strength, mostly because it brought more volume. [R] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6081873/
- **Grgic et al. 2018/2019** (frequency and strength): higher frequency was associated with somewhat larger strength gains, with a small effect that is largely explained by volume. For hypertrophy under equated volume, frequency has no pronounced effect. [R] https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/5665
- **Pelland et al. 2024** dose-response meta-regression (preprint, Zourdos lab): frequency has a consistently identifiable effect on **strength** (a skill/practice effect) but not on hypertrophy once volume is accounted for. [R, preprint] https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460
- **Currier et al. 2023** network meta-analysis (192 studies): moderate loads trained about 2×/week captured about 77% of the maximal standardized strength gains. [R] (summary: https://peterattiamd.com/lowering-the-barrier-to-entry-for-resistance-training/)

### Interpretation for 2–4 sessions/week
- Weekly sets per muscle and per lift drive most of the outcome. For a novice, frequency matters mainly through practice of the specific lift.
- Full-body sessions are the robust choice when frequency varies. At 2 sessions/week, every muscle still gets 2 exposures. Upper/lower would drop each muscle to 1×/week in a 2-session week.
- A/B alternation with **each session containing one squat or hinge plus one press** means:
  - SQ and BP (present in both sessions, heavy in one and lighter in the other) get 2–4 exposures/week.
  - DL and OHP heavy get 1–2 exposures/week.
  - The bench target is the hardest one, so bench gets the extra frequency.
- Tiering (heavy T1, moderate-volume T2, accessories T3) is a coaching convention (GZCL) with good face validity: heavy low-rep work is specific to 1RM, and the lighter work adds volume without much fatigue. [C/E]
- GZCLP's 4-workout rotation (A1/B1/A2/B2) puts each lift at T1 only once per 4 sessions. At 2 sessions/week that is once every 2 weeks, which is too sparse. Prefer a 2-session rotation.

## 2. Progression rules

### Linear progression (LP)
- Typical community increments per successful session:
  - SQ and DL: +5 lb (2.5 kg); DL is often +10 lb (5 kg) early on.
  - BP and OHP: +2.5–5 lb (1.25–2.5 kg).
  - StrongLifts, Greyskull, GZCLP and Reddit PPL all sit in this range. [C]
  - https://liftosaur.com/programs/gzclp
  - https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/metallicadpappl
- OHP and bench outrun 2.5 kg jumps quickly, because 2.5 kg is about 5% of a 50 kg press. Use 1.25 kg jumps with fractional plates. Without them, use rep-based progression (3×5 → 3×6 → 3×7, then add 2.5 kg and drop back to 3×5). [C/E]

### Failure handling in popular programs [C]
- **StrongLifts** (official): failing reps means repeat the weight. Three failed workouts in a row mean −10%. The count and the % are configurable. https://support.stronglifts.com/article/71-increments
- **Greyskull LP** (Phrak):
  - 2×5 + 1×5+ AMRAP.
  - If the AMRAP is under 5 reps, repeat the weight. A second failure means −10%.
  - If the AMRAP is 10 or more reps, double the jump.
  - https://www.boostcamp.app/r-fitness/greyskull-linear-progression
  - https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/phrakgreyskull
- **GZCLP**:
  - T1 runs 5×3+, then 6×2+, then 10×1+, all at the same weight. You move one stage down on each failure. After failing 10×1, retest a 5RM or take −10 to −15% and restart at 5×3.
  - T2 runs 3×10, then 3×8, then 3×6. After failing 3×6, return to the last 3×10 weight + about 7 kg.
  - T3 is 3×15+. Add weight when the last set reaches 25+.
  - Rest times are not specified, apart from 90 s on T3. https://liftosaur.com/programs/gzclp
- The useful mechanic: **change sets×reps at the same load before reducing load**. This stretches LP several weeks longer, because volume falls while intensity stays.

### When to move from session-to-session to slower progression
- No hard research threshold exists. In coaching practice the cue is repeated stalls on a lift despite sleep and food, about 2 resets on the same lift within roughly 6–8 weeks. [E] (Rippetoe/Kilgore's Practical Programming definition of novice vs intermediate. 5/3/1 for Beginners is the common next step.)
- Long-term data show that gain rate falls as strength rises:
  - **Latella et al. 2020**: 1,897 powerlifters over 15 years gained about 0.15 kg/day on the total on average. The weakest quartile of men gained about 0.21 kg/day, the strongest about 0.10 kg/day. [R, observational] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7448836/
  - **McMaster et al. 2013**: adaptation rate drops after about 12–24 months of training. https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2013/788
  - **Steele et al. 2022** (minimal-dose training, n = 14,690): strength plateaued at about 1 year on minimal dose. https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/69
- Make the switch **per lift, not globally**. OHP and bench typically stall first, deadlift last.
- Slower progression options:
  - (a) Double progression on T1 (e.g. 3×4–6).
  - (b) Allow at most one load increase per lift per 7 days.
  - (c) 5/3/1-style training max (TM = 90% e1RM) with a 3-session wave, where the TM rises 2.5 kg (upper) or 5 kg (lower) per completed wave. Run it by sessions, not weeks.
  - (d) nSuns-style: an AMRAP-rep table sets the increase (0–1 reps +0, 2–3 reps +2.5 kg, 4–5 reps +2.5–5 kg, more than 5 reps +5–7.5 kg). https://thefitness.wiki/routines/nsuns-lp/

### e1RM
- Epley: e1RM = load × (1 + reps/30). Adjust for RIR by using reps + RIR. It is reasonable for sets of 10 reps or fewer and best for 1–6 reps. Treat it as a **trend metric**, not a truth. [E]
- Uses:
  - Charts and goal pacing.
  - Stall detection, e.g. e1RM trend flat or falling over the last 3 exposures.
  - Setting T2 load (about 65–75% of T1 e1RM).
  - Re-entry loads.
  - Setting the TM when the lifter switches to intermediate.

### RIR/RPE autoregulation
- **Zourdos et al. 2016** introduced the RIR-based RPE scale (RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9 = 1 RIR, …). RPE correlated strongly with bar velocity in both experienced lifters (r = −0.88) and novices (r = −0.77). Experienced lifters rated near-maximal loads more accurately. [R] https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/efef3b25-6701-4fb5-bb82-55fcd2a26027/full
- **Helms et al. 2018**: in trained men over 8 weeks, RPE-based load selection performed as well as or slightly better than %1RM, with a small, probable 1RM advantage for squat. [R] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5877330/
- **Halperin et al. 2022** (meta-analysis, 414 participants): people underestimate reps to failure by about 1 rep on average, with large individual variation. Accuracy is better near failure and in sets of 12 reps or fewer. Training status was not a significant moderator. [R] https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/67
- **Robinson et al. 2024** (proximity-to-failure meta-regression, Sports Med): strength gains are similar whether sets stop near failure or several reps short. Hypertrophy benefits modestly from going closer to failure. [R] https://www.fau.edu/newsdesk/articles/muscle-growth-strength-study (paper DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2)
- **Implication: easy / ok / grinder / failed on the last set is a sensible 4-point RIR proxy.**
  - easy ≈ RIR 3+
  - ok ≈ RIR 1–2
  - grinder ≈ RIR 0, with slow reps
  - failed = missed reps
- It beats binary success/fail because:
  - It lets the app take bigger jumps when the lifter is far from limit (as Greyskull does with AMRAP ≥10).
  - It slows progression *before* failure, which reduces failed sessions and resets.
  - It does not require the lifter to train to failure, which Robinson shows is unnecessary for strength.
- The coarse 4-level scale suits novices, who are within about 1–2 reps when far from failure (Halperin). The specific mapping is a design choice [E/C]; research supports the concept [R].
- An optional AMRAP last set (Greyskull/GZCLP) gives an objective cross-check every few sessions.

### Double progression for accessories [C/E]
- Hypertrophy is similar across a wide range of loads when sets go close to failure (Currier 2023; Robinson 2024). The rep range can therefore be chosen for comfort and joint friendliness [R].
- Double progression itself is a coaching convention [C].
- Rule:
  - Set a range (e.g. 3×8–12).
  - When **all** sets hit the top of the range at "ok" or easier, add the smallest increment (dumbbell +1–2 kg, machine/cable one pin or +2.5–5 kg, barbell +2.5 kg) and restart at the bottom.
  - If reps fall below the bottom on 2 sessions, cut the load 5–10%.
- GZCLP's T3 variant: 3×15+, and add load when the AMRAP reaches 25 or more.

## 3. Returning after a gap (detraining)

- **Bosquet et al. 2013** (Scand J Med Sci Sports, 103 studies):
  - Detraining effects follow a dose-response with duration.
  - Maximal strength was largely preserved (trivial effect) for up to about 4 weeks, with losses accelerating after that.
  - Losses were larger in people over 65 and in inactive people.
  - Strength endurance and submaximal performance fell sooner than maximal force.
  - [R] DOI 10.1111/sms.12047; summary https://www.strongerbyscience.com/detraining/
- **McMaster et al. 2013** (elite rugby/football): about −14.5% strength after detraining periods averaging about 7 weeks. [R] https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2013/788
- **Ogasawara et al. 2013**: 3-week breaks inserted every 6 weeks over 24 weeks gave similar strength and muscle CSA gains to continuous training. Retraining caught up fast. [R] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-012-2511-9
- **2024 Jyväskylä study** ("Does taking a break matter"): a 10-week break did not compromise long-term adaptations versus continuous training. [R] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39364857/
- **Blocquiaux et al. 2020** (older men): 12 weeks of detraining cost about 5–15% of strength, and pre-break 1RM was regained in under 8 weeks of retraining. [R] https://researchportal.be/en/node/115961
- **Coleman et al. 2024**: complete cessation for 1 week mid-program slightly blunted lower-body strength gains in trained lifters (no effect on hypertrophy). [R] https://peerj.com/articles/16777
- **Nuckols (Stronger by Science) practical guidance**:
  - Layoff under 1 month: treat it as a deload and drop about 20% in the first week back.
  - Layoff of 1 month or more: start at about one-third of prior loads and rebuild.
  - Regain time is about half the layoff length.
  - [E] https://www.strongerbyscience.com/detraining/
- **Key insight for the app:** after 1–3 weeks off, true strength is barely lost. The load drop is there to manage DOMS, re-groove technique and tissue tolerance, not to rebuild lost strength. That means the drop can be small and the climb back fast (double increments up to the previous working weight).
- Painter's note: a manual job maintains general work capacity, but not barbell-specific tolerance or skill.

### Proposed re-entry table [E/C; anchored by R]
Days since **that lift** was last trained (not since the last session):

| Days off | T1 load | Climb back |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 7 | normal progression | — |
| 8–13 | repeat last load (no increment) | normal |
| 14–20 | −10% | double increments while below previous working load and rated ok/easy |
| 21–34 | −15% | same |
| 35–60 | −25% | same; T2/T3 −20% |
| > 60 | −40%, or run a "re-test" session (work up to a 5-rep set at RIR 2) | double increments; reaching the old load takes about half the layoff |

- After any gap of 8 days or more, failure and stall counters reset and the deload timer resets.
- A missed day never counts as a failure.

## 4. Deloads

- **Bell et al. 2023 Delphi** (Sports Med Open; 21–34 coaches) [E]:
  - Deload defined as a period of reduced training stress to manage fatigue and prepare for further training.
  - It mostly reduces volume, with intensity reduced moderately or kept.
  - It typically lasts 5–7 days.
  - It is used both planned and reactively.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/
- **Bell et al. 2022** (coach interviews): deloads every 4–6 weeks for about 5–7 days, with high variability. [E] https://doaj.org/article/f1457672bb654bc3b363b5caa64815b3
- **Bell et al. 2025** practical recommendations: https://shura.shu.ac.uk/35313/
- **Coleman et al. 2024**: the first experimental deload study. One week of *complete rest* gave slightly worse strength outcomes than continuing. It does not test reduced-volume deloads. [R] https://peerj.com/articles/16777
- No RCT shows that novices on LP need planned deloads. In LP programs, resets *are* the reactive deload [C].
- For this lifter, natural work gaps of 1–2 weeks will often act as deloads by themselves.

### Proposed rule
- **Novice phase:** no planned deloads.
- **Reactive trigger** (any one of):
  - (a) 2 or more different T1 lifts rated grinder/failed in the last 3 sessions.
  - (b) 2 resets within 14 days.
  - (c) The user taps "beat up / poor sleep" in 2 consecutive sessions.
- **Intermediate phase:** additionally, offer a deload after about 6 weeks (or about 15 sessions) without any 7-day-plus gap. Skip it if a gap happened.
- **Deload execution:** 2–3 sessions (about 1 week). Keep loads at about 90% and halve the sets (T1 2 sets, T2 2 sets, T3 1–2 sets). Don't skip the gym entirely: Coleman found full cessation slightly worse.

## 5. Pull-ups: from 3 reps to 20

- **Evidence is thin.** There are no good RCTs comparing grease-the-groove (GTG), weighted, or volume approaches for max reps. What exists:
  - The Mountain Tactical Institute informal study, where volume and eccentric groups progressed faster than the weighted group. [weak] https://mtntactical.com/?p=63437
  - A 2009 JSCR study in women: lat-pull 1RM and pull-up reps were not interchangeable, so practice the pull-up itself for specificity. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19620915/
  - General principles apply [R by analogy]:
    - Muscular endurance at bodyweight improves with volume and with raising max strength. Pull-ups at bodyweight are then a lower % of 1RM.
    - Bodyweight is a major determinant.
- **Armstrong Pull-up Program** (USMC) [C]:
  - 5 days/week: max sets / pyramid / grip variety / max training sets / repeat hardest.
  - Morning push-ups.
  - Too frequent for this lifter, but the pyramid and "training sets at a fixed submax rep" ideas are useful.
  - https://liftvault.com/programs/bodyweight/armstrong-pull-up-program-spreadsheet/
- **Grease-the-groove** (Pavel Tsatsouline) [C/E]:
  - Many submaximal sets (about 40–50% of max) spread through the day, never to failure.
  - Plausible via practice frequency (Pelland: frequency → strength).
  - Fits a home doorframe bar on non-gym days.

### Proposed plan
- **Phase 1 (max 1–7):** each gym session, a rep target = 3× current max, done in sets of (max − 1) with 2–3 min rest. Add 1–2 negatives (5-s lowering) if the target is unmet. +1–2 reps on the session target when rated ok/easy.
- **Phase 2 (max 8–12):**
  - A session: volume (sets of about 60–70% of max to total 3–4× max).
  - B session: weighted 3–4×3–5 at RIR 2, using double progression on added kg.
- **Phase 3 (max 13–20):**
  - Volume session: ladders 1-2-3-4-5 repeated, or density (as many reps as possible in 8–10 min of submax sets).
  - Keep weighted 1×/week.
- **Test max** as the first pull-up set every 3rd pull-up session, or use an AMRAP last set.
- **Optional GTG** on non-gym days: 3–6 sets of 40–50% of max.
- **Expected pace:** about +1 rep/1–2 weeks early, slowing to about +1 rep/month near the top. 20 strict reps is plausible within 2 years at a lean bodyweight (≤ about 85 kg), hard if he bulks for bench.

A Rippetoe/Kilgore-style tier summary (secondary) puts a male intermediate squat at about 1.5×BW and advanced at about 2.25×BW: https://www.sensai.fit/blog/strength-standards-by-age-bodyweight

Typical lift ratios in community data:
- BP is about 0.70–0.75 of SQ.
- DL is about 1.15–1.25 of SQ.
- OHP is about 0.60–0.65 of BP.

His targets imply BP/SQ = 0.84, DL/SQ = 1.35 and OHP/BP = 0.62. The squat target is the conservative one; bench and deadlift are aggressive relative to it.

Verdict, assuming BW 80–90 kg:
- **SQ 155** (1.7–1.9×BW) is about "intermediate" and **on pace**. A consistent trainee often reaches it in 12–24 months. It may be exceeded.
- **BP 130** (1.45–1.6×BW) is "advanced" at 80 kg and between intermediate and advanced at 90 kg. **Biggest stretch.** Probably needs BW of 90 kg or more and high bench frequency. Realistic 2-year range: 100–120 kg.
- **DL 210** (2.3–2.6×BW) is near "advanced". **Stretch but possible.** The deadlift grows fastest in absolute kg. Realistic range: 180–210 kg.
- **OHP 80** (0.9–1.0×BW) is "advanced" at 80 kg. **Stretch.** Realistic range: 65–80 kg.
- **20 pull-ups:** plausible if he stays lean. This goal conflicts with gaining weight for bench.
- At BW of 100 kg or more, all barbell targets move toward "intermediate-advanced" and become more realistic. Pull-ups get harder.

Pace check:
- From an estimated current total of about 190 kg (SQ 65 + BP 55 + DL 70) to 495 kg means about +305 kg in about 670 days, roughly 0.45 kg/day.
- That is about 2× the fastest quartile in Latella's competitive powerlifters (0.21 kg/day).
- It is feasible only because novice gains are very front-loaded. The first 6–9 months of LP can add 150+ kg to the total. After that, expect about 50–80 kg/year.
- **Suggestion:** the app should show pace per lift against a decelerating curve, not a straight line.

## 8. Warm-ups and rest

### Rest intervals
- **Schoenfeld et al. 2016** (JSCR, trained men, 8 weeks): 3-min rest gave more strength (squat, bench) and more quad thickness than 1-min rest. [R] DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001272
- **Grgic et al. 2018** (Sports Med, systematic review, strength):
  - Trained lifters: more than 2 min maximizes strength.
  - Untrained lifters: 60–120 s may suffice.
  - Short rest still gives substantial gains.
  - [R] https://vuir.vu.edu.au/38537/
- **Singer et al. 2024** (Frontiers, Bayesian meta-analysis, hypertrophy): a small benefit for rest over 60 s and little apparent extra benefit beyond about 90 s. Only 9 studies, mostly in untrained men. [R] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/
- **Rest timer defaults:**
  - T1: 3 min (allow 3–5 min after a grinder).
  - T2: 2 min.
  - T3: 90 s.
  - Pull-up volume sets: 2 min.
  - Weighted pull-ups: 3 min.
  - Warm-ups: 30–90 s, rising with load.
  - [R for direction, E for exact numbers]

### Warm-ups
- **Ribeiro et al. 2020** (IJERPH) and the 2021 systematic review (Motricidade): specific warm-up sets help. Including a set close to working load improves performance and velocity in the first work set, while the evidence on volume and structure is mixed. [R, modest]
  - https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jijerp/v17y2020i18p6882-d417011.html
  - https://doaj.org/article/25ec6f05b30f409c83052eb328c0d9f7
- **App ramp:**
  - Empty bar ×10 and ×5, then about 40% ×5, 60% ×3, 80% ×2 of the work weight.
  - Skip steps below 20 kg or within 10 kg of each other.
  - Deadlift starts at about 60 kg or 40%.
  - T2 lifts of a pattern already warmed up get 1 ramp set.
  - [E/C]
